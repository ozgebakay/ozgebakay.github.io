Hello! I am a fifth-year PhD student in Linguistics at the University of Massachusetts Amherst, expected to graduate in September 2026.

I am a psycholinguist, specializing on how people understand language in real-time. I am also interested in investigating how this is done using computational models.

Before coming to UMass, I got my BA and MA from the Foreign Language Education Department at Boğaziçi University in İstanbul, Turkey. My MA thesis was on Processing Turkish center-embeddings: an investigation of case interference and prosodic phrase lengths.

In my dissertation supervised by Brian Dillon and Faruk Akkuş, I examine (i) whether item-to-item hierarchical relations (e.g., c-command) become rapidly available as a cue in memory access and (ii) if so, how they are encoded within the memory architecture subserving real-time sentence processing. Empirically, I demonstrate evidence from a set of visual world experiments on the Turkish reciprocal that hierarhical structural information guides the earliest stages of memory access. Theoretically, I model these findings by proposing that linguistic items are encoded and accessed in memory via their hierarchical structural positions.
