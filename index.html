Hello!

I am a fifth-year PhD student in Linguistics at the University of Massachusetts Amherst, expected to graduate in September 2026. In October 2026, I will be joining Jon Brennan's lab at the University of Michigan as a postdoc researcher!

I am a psycholinguist, specializing on how people understand language in real-time. I am also interested in modelling how this is done using computational models.

Before coming to UMass, I got my BA and MA from the Foreign Language Education Department at Boğaziçi University in İstanbul, Turkey. I earned my MA degree upon completion of a thesis titled as Processing Turkish center-embeddings: an investigation of case interference and prosodic phrase lengths.

In my dissertation project supervised by Brian Dillon and Faruk Akkuş, I examine (i) whether item-to-item hierarchical relations (e.g., c-command) become rapidly available as a cue in memory access and (ii) if so, how they are encoded within the memory architecture subserving real-time sentence processing. Empirically, I demonstrate evidence from a set of visual world experiments on the Turkish local reciprocal that hierarhically available items are robustly available in the earliest stages of memory access. I model these findings by adopting structurally-oriented models of working memory where linguistic items are encoded and accessed via their hierarchical structural positions. See my recent JML paper on work related to this here!
