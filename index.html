Hello!

I am a fifth-year PhD student in Linguistics at the University of Massachusetts Amherst, expected to graduate in September 2026.

I am a psycholinguist, specializing on how people understand language in real-time. I am also interested in investigating how they do so using computational models.

Before coming to UMass, I got my BA and MA from the Foreign Language Education Department at Boğaziçi University in İstanbul, Turkey. My MA thesis was on Processing Turkish center-embeddings: an investigation of case interference and prosodic phrase lengths.

In my dissertation supervised by Brian Dillon and Faruk Akkuş, I examine (i) whether item-to-item hierarchical relations (e.g., c-command) constrain initial memory access and (ii) how relationally available (e.g., c-commanding) items are made rapidly available for memory access. Empirically, I show evidence from a set of visual world experiments that relationally available target referents are reactivated more robustly and more rapidly than unavailable distractors during the resolution of the Turkish local reciprocal birbirleri `each other'. Theoretically, I propose that c-commanding items are selectively maintained in a privileged focal state for direct access—an account grounded in position-based architectures where linguistic elements are encoded and accessed via their hierarchical positional information.
